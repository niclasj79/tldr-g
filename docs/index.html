See it in three minutes

Three questions about a made-up lender's documents, and with each answer, one thing you can check: the sources it used, whether the decision still holds, and a signed receipt of what the model was given, verified offline.

Finding the document was never the hard part

Every document was findable. The hard part is knowing which one decides the question: which version applied, what replaced it, where the decision is written down. A pipeline that hands the model the most similar chunks can get you yesterday's decision, quoted perfectly, with nothing to show what the model was given.

TLDR-G picks the evidence a question needs, shows you what it picked, and keeps a signed record of it. So you can check an answer instead of trusting it.

How it works

Know what matters

Your documents become a knowledge graph: passages, and the names and relations in them, each tied to the exact text it came from. For each question the engine selects the evidence and fits it under a hard token budget, a ceiling and never a target.

See what changed

Every answer lists the passages it was given and opens each one at its source. When a decision was later reversed, you read the line that reversed it, in the document itself.

Prove why

Each answer gets a signed receipt: the question and fingerprints of the evidence, signed before any model is asked. Anyone can check it offline with a standalone verifier. It proves what went in; whether the answer is right stays your reviewers' call.

Proof, not adjectives

  • On a small legal set (five public filings, 18 questions, one model, May 2026), the rendered context matched full-context answer quality at about 12K instead of 104K tokens. One corpus: it shows the mechanism works, and it is not a comparison with any product.
  • Runs on your machine. The whole knowledge graph is one local file (SQLite).
  • A signed receipt for every answer, and a verifier that needs no server and no account.
  • The source behind every passage, one click away.

Sovereign by construction

Your documents, and the index built from them, should not depend on someone else's cloud staying available or keeping its terms.

Local-first

The engine and the knowledge graph run on your hardware. The app shows, on every page, where your evidence is sent.

Your choice of model

Answers can come from a local model or a cloud model you choose. Or take just the context, with no model at all.

Inspectable

The receipt formats and the verifier are open source, so you can check our work without trusting us.

Free engine. Open SDK. Not open-core.

The engine is free to run, distributed as a compiled binary. The SDK — the integration contracts and the verification surface — is open source under Apache-2.0, so you can build against a stable boundary and verify everything for yourself. We're not open-core: the engine stays closed; the contracts you depend on stay open.

And we're building an honest way for it to get better for everyone: an opt-in, content-free signal about how it rendered — never your data, never one user exposed to another — that improves the shared defaults. It's off by default, inspectable, and won't switch on until its privacy guarantee is provable. We apply the transparency we sell to ourselves.

The engine: free download

The Cockpit desktop app, the tldr-g-mcp server for your AI tools, and an HTTP API. Free to use.

Requirements (v0.3.2): Windows 10/11 (64-bit). An NVIDIA GPU with ≥4 GB VRAM is strongly recommended (GTX 1060 6 GB or better); it runs on CPU only, but ingest and questions are substantially slower there. 16 GB RAM recommended. About 12 GB of free disk: the installer is 1.9 GB, it unpacks to about 5 GB, and about 4.6 GB of models download once on first launch. macOS and Linux: fast-follow.
